01 / HARDWARELOCAL SIM

32-bit pipelined CPU

Integrated a five-stage Verilog processor with an 18-instruction course ISA, signed Booth multiplication, and restoring division.

23/23local simulation suites passed
Architecture & verification

Fetch, decode, execute, memory, and writeback connect the ALU, register file, instruction ROM, data RAM, and mult/div unit. I added operand forwarding, a one-cycle load-use interlock, a late store-data bypass, and branch/jump flushing.

A sequential reference model checked register writes, stores, final register and memory state, and expected stalls. The 23 local suites include directed hazards and random programs. FPGA timing and official course checks are unverified.

The RTL samples below are earlier component checkpoints. This case study describes the later integrated processor.

02 / CONTROLSREAL FLIGHT

Crazyflie flight tuning

Tuned horizontal PID gains in simulation, transferred them to a physical Crazyflie, and analyzed recorded waypoint flights against a baseline.

−0.900 sleg 2 time versus baseline
Flight data & tradeoffs

I split telemetry by waypoint, compared rise time, overshoot, and tracking error, and used a short moving average to compare filtered and raw position.

Leg 2 took 1.202 s versus 2.102 s for the baseline. That is a single leg, not a course-wide speedup: the complete course took 19.599 s versus 19.300 s, within the lab’s stated baseline variation. Neither flight skipped a waypoint.

03 / PERCEPTIONSIM TRIALS

TurtleBot sensor testing

Measured ROS 2 sensor rates and noise, corrected the lidar frame offset, and tuned stop thresholds for four obstacle scenarios in simulation.

8/8final simulated runs met lane targets
Sensor choice & validation

A lidar-only run missed the low box. The final configuration used depth for the box, cliff detection for the pit, lidar for the wall, and a color threshold for the red floor zone.

I tested each lane at 0.20 and 0.30 m/s. Travel after the stop signal increased from 0.024–0.030 m to 0.052–0.063 m at the higher speed. The result applies to these simulated trials.

MAP / PLAN / MOVE05

Nav2 exploration trials

Ran TurtleBot exploration in Gazebo with SLAM and Nav2. Compared what happened when goals fell in known free space versus unexplored map cells.

What the logs showed

One unchecked goal in an unknown cell timed out, while a later goal in free space succeeded. I used the logs to examine goal selection and navigation failure, using the course’s navigation stack.
